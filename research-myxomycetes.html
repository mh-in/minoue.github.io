Research · Topic

Slime moulds (Myxomycetes)

Myxomycetes (Amoebozoa) are a group of protists, comprising more than 1,050 described species1. They are almost cosmopolitan in distribution and often abundant on the upper soil layer, yet their ecological roles remain relatively poorly understood.

My research focuses on the phylogeography and genetic diversity of nivicolous myxomycetes — species that form fruiting bodies near snowbanks shortly after the seasonal snow cover (typically lasting over three months) has melted.

This ecological guild is particularly suitable for quantitative studies, as seasonal sampling is predictable, and most species belong to the dark-spored clade, which is particularly well-suited for molecular identification using the nearly universal 18S rDNA barcoding marker2,3,4.

Although these organisms cannot yet be cultivated under laboratory conditions, molecular data from field-collected specimens provide valuable insights into the reproductive strategies of free-dispersing protists and patterns of local adaptation from an evolutionary biology perspective.

Key findings so far

  • Fruiting depends on stable snow. Using a transect survey in the German Alps maintained by colleagues for about ten years, I combined barcoding-based identification and fruiting-body records with on-site temperature loggers and satellite-derived environmental data. Fruiting abundance correlated strongly with the length of the snow season and with the longest continuous period suitable for growth under snow, and inversely with soil frost. Nivicolous myxomycetes appear to persist as trophic or resting stages regardless of snow cover, but fruit only in years and at elevations with stable snow cover4. Inoue et al. 2024, Fungal Ecology · first author
  • Regional endemism instead of unlimited gene flow. Together with 15 collaborators, I analysed more than 5,500 barcodes from quantitative surveys across 11 Eurasian mountain ranges. Community dissimilarity increased strongly with geographic distance, even after controlling for climate, indicating dispersal limitation; phylogenetic composition, by contrast, was better explained by climate. Geographically isolated regions — the central Japanese mountains, the Sierra Nevada, and Kamchatka — harboured particularly high proportions of endemic genotypes, challenging the “everything is everywhere” paradigm5. Inoue et al. 2026, Ecosphere (in press) · first and corresponding author

Current projects

Collaborative work

I am also involved in several collaborative projects on nivicolous myxomycetes in various mountain ranges, as well as on taxonomy, to which I contribute by conducting investigations and analysing data.

Within the research group

  • Nivicolous myxomycetes in the French Pyrenees. Investigation · diversity data analysis · writing (review & editing)
  • Population genetics of Diderma meyerae across European mountains. Investigation · data curation
  • Nivicolous myxomycetes in Macedonia, Romania, the French Alps, and the German Alps (Berchtesgaden). Data curation · data evaluation
  • Taxonomic works: Didymium dubium species complex, Meriderma spp., Badhamia almatensis, Diderma brooksii, Tubifera spp., Reticularia spp., Lamproderma group. Investigation · data curation · data evaluation
  • Biogeography of corticolous myxomycetes in Germany. Investigation · data analysis

With external colleagues

  • Long-term research on nivicolous myxomycetes in the German Alps and the Tatra Mountains — with Dr. A. Ronikier and Dr. P. Janik, Polish Academy of Sciences. Joint investigation · data curation · molecular data analysis · morphological identification
  • Nivicolous myxomycetes in Kazakhstan — with PhD cand. Z. Azirakhmet, L.N. Gumilyov Eurasian National University. Molecular data analysis · morphological identification · writing (review & editing)
  • Corticolous myxomycetes in Japan — with Dr. K. Fujioka. Data curation · molecular data analysis
  • Genetic diversity of myxomycetes in East Asia — with a Master’s student at National Taiwan University, Dr. Y. Yajima (Muroran Institute of Technology), and many independent researchers in Japan and Taiwan. Joint investigation · data curation · molecular data analysis · morphological identification
